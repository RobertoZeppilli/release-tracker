02

And when the notes are not that kind

Most releases do not publish 271 lines. What Verdict does with the thin ones is the same discipline, which is why it is on this page and not in a FAQ.

  • Vite 6.0.0 a changelog of 28 lines, where the two breaking ones are marked by a single ! and sit between a chore and a perf. read them apart
  • TypeScript 5.7.2 a release whose body is a link to a blog post. There is nothing here to judge, so Verdict says exactly that and points at where the real notes live. see that state

note: not enough notes to judge

This is the sentence Verdict prints instead of a verdict when the notes only point elsewhere. “I found no breaking changes” and “there are no breaking changes” are different claims, and the second one is never printed in place of the first.

03

Four words on every release

Significance is a shape beside the version, never a hue: colour on this product means severity and nothing else. A release is not urgent because it is red, it is urgent because its notes say so, on a line you can read.

featured
The release where a technology changes direction: a new major where SemVer holds, a new minor for the projects that ship their real changes there. These open your dashboard.
notable
New work, no change of direction: a new minor under SemVer, an odd-numbered Node major. Worth reading, rarely urgent.
digest
What the rule treats as routine, patches first. They never interrupt: you read them grouped by ISO week, in one pass.
preview
A beta or a release candidate. Only the first preview of a new major is summarized, and canaries and nightlies are not releases here at all.
